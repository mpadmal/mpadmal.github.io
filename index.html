About

Madhushanka Padmal is a wireless systems researcher and RF/hardware engineer who recently completed a PhD in Electrical Engineering at Uppsala University, focused on ultra-low-power wireless systems: backscatter communication, RF sensing, FMCW radar, and secure and covert communication networks. His work spans microwave and RF circuit design, antenna and electromagnetic simulation, embedded firmware, and RF measurement, taking designs from concept and architecture through prototyping to production.

He has authored 20+ peer-reviewed publications, including two Best-Paper awards and a patent-pending tunnel-diode transmitter recognized among the Top 30 innovations in Sweden (IVA, 2025). Alongside research, he leads several open-source hardware platforms and has mentored 50+ contributors through Google Summer of Code. He works fluently across international, multidisciplinary teams and is currently based in Uppsala, Sweden.

Selected Research & Projects

SharpPeak — Tunnel-Diode Transmitter

Patent-pending · ACM SenSys 2026 · IVA Top 30

Ultra-low-power long-range active transmitter at <175 µW with up to 370 kbps and >1 km range. Novel two-stage biasing for stable, low-phase-noise oscillation without injection locking.

MicrowaveOscillator designPhase noisePatent

Chord — Multi-Tone Backscatter Querying

DCOSS-IoT 2026 · First author

O(log N) querying for analog backscatter using multi-tone excitation, combinatorial SAW-filter arrays, and a logic-gate discriminator. Fully analog, no digital processing.

SAW filtersBackscatterRF front-end

RF Source Localization with Backscatter

ACM MSWiM 2025 · First author

First analog-backscatter system letting multiple tags sense the channel concurrently to localize and track an ambient RF source, with ~20–55 cm accuracy and low-cost SDR receivers.

LocalizationPath-loss modelUSRP

Fat-IBC Covert Communication

IEEE WoWMoM 2025 · First author

First covert in-body communication, using tissue attenuation and a friendly jammer to hide implant links from external eavesdroppers. Detection-and-estimation-theory analysis plus phantom experiments.

PHY securityIn-body / WBANCST Studio

FatSim — In-Body Channel Simulator

ACM MSWiM 2026 · First author

First network-level simulator for fat-tissue in-body communication: a COOJA RadioMedium plugin with image-method ray tracing, calibrated to CST field data (RMSE 2.2 dB).

Ray tracingEM modelingJava / COOJA

RFID Tags as Temperature Sensors

IEEE RFID 2023 · First author

Reuses commodity RFID tags as non-invasive temperature sensors by modeling permittivity-to-RSSI behavior. 22–60 °C range, 2 °C accuracy, no line-of-sight required.

RFIDRF sensingAntenna sim
